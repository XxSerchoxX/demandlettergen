How to Write a Demand Letter: A Step-by-Step Guide

A demand letter is one of the most effective tools to resolve disputes without going to court. This guide walks you through exactly what to include, how to phrase it, and how to send it for maximum impact.

What Is a Demand Letter?

A demand letter is a formal written notice sent to an individual or business requesting that they fulfill a legal obligation — typically paying a debt, issuing a refund, or repairing damages — within a specific deadline.

Courts in the United States often expect that you made a good-faith attempt to resolve a dispute before filing a lawsuit. A demand letter creates that paper trail and frequently resolves issues without any legal action.

Step-by-Step: How to Write a Demand Letter

01

Include your contact information and the date

At the top of the letter, write your full name, address, phone number, and email. Below that, add the date. This creates a formal record of when the demand was made.

02

Address the recipient clearly

Include the recipient's full name (or company name) and address. If you know the specific person responsible, address them directly rather than using a generic greeting.

03

Write a clear subject line

Use a direct subject line such as "Demand for Payment" or "Formal Demand Letter Re: Invoice #1234". This signals the seriousness of the letter immediately.

04

State the facts chronologically

Describe what happened in factual, unemotional terms. Include dates, amounts, and any relevant agreements. Avoid personal attacks or emotional language — courts respond to facts, not feelings.

05

State your demand clearly

Be specific about what you want: the exact dollar amount, a specific action, or both. Vague demands are easier to ignore or dispute.

06

Set a firm deadline

Give the recipient a specific date to respond or pay — typically 14 to 30 days from the date of the letter. A deadline creates urgency and gives you a clear timeline for next steps.

07

State the consequences

Clearly explain what will happen if your demand is not met. Common consequences include filing in small claims court, hiring an attorney, reporting to credit agencies, or pursuing collections.

08

Close professionally and sign

End with a professional closing such as "Sincerely" followed by your signature, printed name, and contact information.

How to Send a Demand Letter

The method of delivery matters legally. For the strongest documentation:

  • Certified mail with return receipt — creates a legal record that the recipient received the letter
  • Email with read receipt — acceptable for less formal disputes, but less reliable as legal proof
  • Both — sending via both methods is the safest approach for high-value disputes

Important: Keep a copy of the letter and proof of delivery. You may need these documents if you proceed to court.

What to Do If They Don't Respond

If the deadline passes without a response or payment, your options include:

  • Filing a claim in small claims court (for amounts typically under $10,000–$15,000 depending on your state)
  • Hiring an attorney to pursue civil litigation
  • Using a debt collection agency
  • Reporting the debt to credit bureaus (if applicable)

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer to write a demand letter?
No. Anyone can write and send a demand letter. However, for complex disputes or large amounts, having an attorney review the letter can strengthen your position.
How long should a demand letter be?
One to two pages is ideal. Long letters dilute your message. Be concise, factual, and direct.
What if the other party disputes my claims?
If they respond with a counter-claim or denial, you can either negotiate a settlement or proceed to small claims court where a judge will decide.
Can a demand letter hurt my case?
A poorly written letter can. Avoid threats of illegal action, false statements, or emotional language. Stick to facts and legal remedies.
